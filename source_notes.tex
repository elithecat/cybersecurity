\section*{Overview}
The deck \texttt{cybersecurity\_2026\_briefing.pdf} contains 4 kinds of claims.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Checked claims.} On November 12, 2026, we downloaded the source page and matched the quoted words or the number in its text. Table 1 lists these claims. The slides show them without a flag.
\item \textbf{Unchecked claims.} The network rules of the session blocked most news, government, and vendor sites. For these claims, the research agents had only the excerpts that the search tool returned. The slides mark each such claim with ``[verify]''. Table 2 lists them by slide, with the research report that holds the URLs.
\item \textbf{Local facts.} An earlier draft marked local facts (the talk date and Brown-specific details) with blue brackets. Alyssa removed them on November 18, 2026. 1 blue bracket remains, for Wirecutter's VPN pick. Table 3 lists what the earlier draft left open.
\item \textbf{Definitions, advice, and conclusions without a source.} These statements are ours: the definitions of terms, the takeaways, the 3-level sort, the time estimates, and general advice (for example ``cover the screen,'' the description of a passkey as a secret and a lock, the table of factors and the statement that a passkey counts as 2 factors, the advice on passkey storage, the comparison of a passcode with face or fingerprint (2 of its statements carry a flag), the limits of a passkey, the lab data steps, the first-hour steps, the step to delete old accounts, and the API key steps). The slides show them without a flag. No table lists them.
\end{enumerate}
We could reach only these hosts: \texttt{www.anthropic.com}, \texttt{cloud.google.com}, \texttt{www.microsoft.com}, \texttt{code.claude.com}, and \texttt{raw.githubusercontent.com}. Many checked claims therefore come from Anthropic, which sells the models that the claims describe. The slide ``Skeptics'' states this limit.

\section*{Known conflicts between sources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Altman's Stanford quote: The Next Web prints ``going to''; Gizmodo prints ``gonna.''
\item Vanderbilt: one search summary says that classes continued; a research report says that the university cancelled classes. The slides do not state either.
\item Stolen passwords on the Sophos slide: the source term is ``compromised credentials.'' The slides say ``stolen passwords.''
\item The Sophos figure of 69\% for restored backups may apply only to organizations whose data the attackers encrypted. We could not check the denominator.
\item Binance: Binance says about \$6.3 billion; TRM Labs says \$6.36 billion; Elliptic says \$6.43 billion.
\item Firefox: Amodei reportedly said ``almost 300'' flaws; Anthropic's page says that Mozilla fixed 271.
\item Stanford interview: the video was reportedly posted on October 21, 2026. We could not confirm the recording date.
\end{itemize}
